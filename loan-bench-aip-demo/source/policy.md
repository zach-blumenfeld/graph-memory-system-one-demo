# Lantern Mutual small-business term loan policy

This policy covers one product: the small-business term loan. Every application is decided from
the application form alone. There are two kinds of rule. A hard rule is a fact on the form
checked against a number in this policy. A judgment rule is a statement about what the applicant
wrote, which a reader must judge.

## Completeness

An application is complete when every field in sections 1 to 3 of the form has a value and all
three required documents are listed in section 4:

- Business tax returns, last two years
- Business bank statements, last six months
- Government-issued ID of the guarantor

An incomplete application is returned to the applicant. No other rule is applied to it.

## Hard rules

Each hard rule passes or fails. There is no middle band.

| Rule | Name | Passes when |
|---|---|---|
| H1 | Amount | The amount requested is from $10,000 to $250,000 |
| H2 | Term | The term is from 12 to 60 months |
| H3 | Time in business | The business has operated for 24 months or more on the date the application was received |
| H4 | Credit score | The guarantor's credit score is 660 or higher |
| H5 | Bankruptcy and liens | No bankruptcy in the last 7 years and no unpaid tax liens |
| H6 | Coverage | Average monthly net income divided by (existing monthly debt payments plus the quoted monthly payment) is 1.25 or higher |
| H7 | Size | The amount requested is 25% or less of annual revenue |

An application that fails any hard rule is declined. The decline names every hard rule that
failed, with the figure and the threshold.

## Judgment rules

Each judgment rule is a statement that holds or does not hold for the application. They are
judged only when the application is complete and passes every hard rule.

### J1 Eligible purpose

Every use of the money described in section 5 is a business use: equipment, inventory, working
capital, fitting out or expanding premises, hiring, or refinancing the business's own debt. None
of it goes to the owner or to anything outside the business: paying personal debts, paying the
owners a draw or dividend, buying out an owner, investments, or property the business will not
occupy.

### J2 Amount matches the use

The uses described in section 5 account for the amount requested. The itemised costs add up to
about the amount requested (within 10%), and no large part of the money is left without a
stated use.

### J3 Adverse items are explained

Every adverse item in the application has an explanation that names a specific cause and shows
it is over. Adverse items are late payments declared in section 3 and any loss, drop in revenue
or closure the applicant mentions. This rule holds when the application has no adverse item.

### J4 Repaid from operations

The applicant expects to repay the loan from the income of the business as it runs today. The
repayment does not depend on something that has not happened yet: a contract not yet signed, a
sale of the business or of property, an investor, or a grant.

### J5 The story matches the form

What the applicant wrote in sections 5 to 7 agrees with the fields in sections 1 to 3: the kind
of business, how long it has run, its revenue, its debts and the amount requested. Nothing
written contradicts a field.

## How the outcome is formed

The outcome is exactly one of four, tested in this order:

1. **return**: the application is incomplete.
2. **decline**: the application fails one or more hard rules.
3. **approve**: every judgment rule holds, each with a probability above 0.75.
4. **escalate**: anything else. The application goes to a credit officer with the judgment
   rules that did not hold. A judgment rule never declines an application by itself; only a
   credit officer declines for a reason that is not a hard rule.
