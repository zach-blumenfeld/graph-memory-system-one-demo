# How we run an email blast

Owner: Tessera Labs marketing. Last reviewed by the team. This is the page new people get
pointed at; it is how we expect every launch, update and digest email to be done.

## What a blast is

One brief in the Campaign Briefs database becomes one email to one audience segment, sent
through the Labs email programme on the date in the brief. Every brief names a product, a
segment (or a persona), a goal, three key messages, a call to action and a send window.

## The rules, in order of how much trouble they save

1. We only say things that are on the product's approved claims list. No numbers, comparisons
   or promises that are not on it. If the brief asks for something that is not approved, leave
   it out and say so in the log.
2. Every email about a Labs project carries the Labs disclaimer from the brand guide, word for
   word. Drafts get bounced for this more than for anything else.
3. The brand guide's voice rules apply: lead with what the reader can do, one idea per sentence,
   no exclamation marks, exactly one call to action.
4. Nothing is scheduled until the draft has passed the compliance check.
5. Digests and nurture emails are not scheduled by us. Draft them, check them, log them; the
   newsletter team sends them.

## Before you write

Read the brief. Work out what kind of email it is (a launch, an update to an existing release, a
nurture or digest, or an event) and how urgent it is: most briefs are normal, a hotfix or a
security notice is urgent and goes out the same day. If the brief mentions anything legal,
pricing, a partner, or a comparison with another product, flag it for legal review before you
schedule anything.

Pick the audience. The brief usually names a segment; if it only names a persona, pick the
segment for that product that matches the persona. Read the persona notes, they tell you the
tone the reader wants.

## Writing

Three subject lines and a body in markdown. Subject lines under 60 characters, no all-caps
words, none of the spam words in the brand guide. The body covers the three key messages,
ends with the one call to action, and carries the disclaimer.

Run the compliance check on the draft. If it fails, fix what it names and run it again.

## Choosing the subject line

Pick the subject line that is clearest for the persona, then the most curious, and never one
that could read as spam. Say which one you chose and why.

## Scheduling and logging

Schedule the passing draft to the segment for the send window in the brief (09:00 UTC unless
the brief says otherwise). Then log the campaign: what went out, to whom, when, and every
judgment call you made along the way.

## Judgment calls

Whenever you decide something a checklist cannot decide for you (what kind of email this is,
how urgent, whether legal needs to see it, which segment, which subject line, whether a claim
is really on the approved list), log it as a judgment: the question, the options you considered,
your answer, and why. Use `blast decide` for this. The log is how the next person learns.
