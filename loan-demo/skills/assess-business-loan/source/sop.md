# How we assess a business loan

Owner: Lantern Mutual credit team. This is the page new analysts get pointed at; it is how we
expect every business-loan application to be worked, from the first look to the filed decision.

## What an assessment is

One application becomes one decision: approve, counter (a smaller amount or a shorter term),
decline, or refer to a human. Every decision is filed with the rules it applied, the facts it
relied on, and a written rationale a customer or an examiner could read. When you decline
someone you owe them a reason, and the reason has to be something we can point to.

## The rules, in order of how much trouble they save

1. Policy first. Run the policy check before you form a view. It applies the hard rules (KYC
   current, no fraud event in twelve months, coverage floor, exposure cap, connected
   delinquency, tenure) and tells you whether the application can be approved or declined
   without judgment, or needs one. Do not argue with a hard rule in the write-up.
2. Look two hops out. An owner's other businesses are our exposure too. A facility that is
   late at a business one of the owners holds a quarter or more of is never an automatic
   approval, however clean the applicant looks on its own.
3. Only cite what is in the systems. Every figure in the rationale (the coverage ratio, the
   payment, the exposure, days late) comes from a record you can name. Mark each one with the
   record it came from. The check rejects a rationale that quotes a figure the systems do not hold.
4. The judgment step cannot see protected characteristics. The ownership system carries dates
   of birth; they are never passed into the assessment and never mentioned in a rationale.
   Nothing about an owner's age, sex, ethnicity, religion, national origin, marital status or
   disability belongs in the file. Describe the business, not the people.
5. Nothing is filed until it has passed the check and the decision has been recorded with its
   confidence and the procedure revision that produced it.

## Before you start

Read the application: the amount, the term, the purpose, and who asked. Read the business: how
long it has banked with us, which industry, what the relationship manager has noted. Then pull
the five things the policy needs: the cash flow (twelve-month average net inflow against the
payments it would carry), the existing facilities and their payment status, the KYC record and
any fraud events, the owners and everything else they hold, and the policy rules themselves.

Look for precedents: decisions we have already filed on applications of the same product in the
same band. They are not binding, but a rationale that departs from them should say why.

## The checks

- Coverage: twelve-month average monthly net inflow divided by the proposed monthly payment plus
  the payments on the facilities the business already has. 1.25 or better stands on its own;
  1.00 to 1.25 is a judgment; under 1.00 is a decline.
- Exposure: everything we lend to the business and to the businesses its owners hold a quarter or
  more of, including the new amount, stays under the cap or goes to a human.
- Connected delinquency: anything sixty days or more late at a connected business is a review.
- KYC and fraud: overdue KYC or a fraud event in the last twelve months is a decline. A refresh
  that is due is a review.
- Tenure: under two years with us is a review. A decline on a customer of five years or more goes
  to a human before it is filed.

## The judgment, when the rules leave one

When the policy says review, decide three things and say how sure you are: the risk tier of the
business (low, medium, high), whether the connected exposure is material to this decision, and
the outcome (approve, counter, decline). Base them on the figures and the fired rules, the
ownership summary, the tenure, the KYC status, and what the relationship manager has noted about
the business. If you are not sure, say so and refer it; a human reads referred cases.

## The write-up

Write the rationale the way the customer and the examiner will read it: what was asked for, what
the policy found, what you decided and why, every figure marked with the record it came from, and
the precedents you leaned on. State the outcome in the first paragraph. Submit it, run the check,
fix what the check names, run it once more. Then record the decision.

## Judgment calls

Whenever you decide something the policy cannot decide for you (the risk tier, whether a connected
exposure matters, whether a counter-offer is better than a decline, whether a precedent applies,
whether to refer), log it as a judgment: the question, the options you considered, your answer,
and why. Use `loan decide` for this. The log is how the next analyst learns, and how the procedure
gets better.
