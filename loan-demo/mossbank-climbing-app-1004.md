Decision: approve. Mossbank Climbing Gym Ltd [cite: BUS-04] applied for a term loan of 600,000 over 120 months to fit out a second gym in the Mossbank Mill lease [cite: APP-1004]. The policy check returns review on one rule: the coverage ratio of 1.15 sits below the 1.25 floor and above the 1.00 decline line, which is the band the policy leaves to judgment. Every other test is clean: KYC is current [cite: KYC-BUS-04], there are no fraud events, there are no connected businesses, the existing term loan is at 0 days late [cite: FAC-02], and the business has banked with us for 7.3 years [cite: BUS-04]. The application is approved at the amount and term requested, with the risk tier assessed as medium.

## What was asked for

The business requested 600,000 as a term loan over 120 months, with a proposed monthly payment of 7439.14, to fit out a second gym: walls, matting and changing rooms in the Mossbank Mill lease [cite: APP-1004]. Mossbank Climbing is a climbing gym founded in September 2018 that has banked with Lantern Mutual since May 2019, a tenure of 88 months or 7.3 years [cite: BUS-04]. The application was submitted by the majority owner, who holds 70 percent of the business [cite: PER-06]; the other owner holds 30 percent [cite: PER-07].

The relationship manager's note from 2026-09-25 records that the Mossbank Mill lease was signed on 2026-09-20 for ten years with a break at five, that 412 founder memberships had been pre-sold at the time of the visit against a target of 600 before opening, and that the original gym is at capacity on weekday evenings [cite: NOTE-05].

## What was decided and why

Approve, at 600,000 over 120 months. The one fired rule is a judgment rule, and the judgment is that the shortfall from 1.25 to 1.15 is carried by the rest of the file.

The coverage ratio is computed on the cash flow of the existing gym alone and gives no credit to the second site. The existing gym is at capacity on weekday evenings, and 412 founder memberships for the new site were pre-sold before it opened [cite: NOTE-05], so the purpose is growth the current premises cannot absorb rather than a business reaching for cash. The twelve-month average net inflow of 12082.82 is above the twenty-four-month average of 11440.33, so the trend over the period the policy looks at is upward.

The business's net inflow is seasonal, and in the summer months of 2026 the operating account's net ran close to the combined monthly payments [cite: MON-401-2026-06] [cite: MON-401-2026-07] [cite: MON-401-2026-08]. That is the reason the risk tier is medium rather than low. It is not a reason to decline or counter: the operating account holds 142,000 [cite: ACC-401] and the savings account holds 95,000 [cite: ACC-402], together roughly two years of the combined monthly payments, which is more than enough to carry a seasonal trough.

A counter-offer was considered and not made. A shorter term raises the payment and worsens the ratio the policy flagged. A smaller amount would lift the ratio toward the floor but leave the fit-out underfunded, and an unfinished second gym is a worse credit than a finished one at 1.15; the business would fill the gap from the same balances that are its cushion for the trough. Referral was considered and not chosen: the facts are all in the systems, no connected party complicates them, and the question the policy left open is one this write-up can answer.

The risk tier is assessed as medium. Supporting it: the coverage ratio of 1.15 below the floor, the seasonal pattern in the operating account, the execution risk of opening a second site, the fact that a fit-out on leased premises leaves little recoverable security, and the five-year break in a ten-year lease [cite: NOTE-05] inside a 120-month term. Against it: 7.3 years of tenure [cite: BUS-04], a current KYC record [cite: KYC-BUS-04], a clean existing facility [cite: FAC-02], no fraud events, no connected businesses, and liquid balances that cover the trough [cite: ACC-401] [cite: ACC-402]. Connected exposure is not material to this decision because there is none.

The single seven-day late payment on the existing loan [cite: PAY-02-2025-08] was weighed and does not count against the application, for the reason recorded in the relationship manager's note [cite: NOTE-06].

## Precedents

One filed decision exists for a term loan in the review band: DEC-APP-1001-01 [cite: DEC-APP-1001-01], a referral. That case was referred because an owner held a majority of a connected business whose facility with us was ninety days late and deteriorating. The reason it was referred does not arise here: Mossbank Climbing has no connected businesses and no delinquency anywhere. Its review flag was connected delinquency; this one is coverage, which that case did not have to judge. This decision departs from the precedent's outcome for that reason and does not treat it as guidance on the coverage question.
